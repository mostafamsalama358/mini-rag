NORTHBRIDGE SUPPLEMENTS & NUTRACEUTICALS GUIDE (E2E Corpus)
Document ID: NB-SUPP-GUIDE-E2E
Audience: Pharmacists counselling on vitamins/minerals with chronic medicines

=== General rules ===
Ask about prescription drugs before recommending any supplement.
Prefer food-first advice; use supplements to correct documented deficiency or meet a clear indication.
Separate interacting minerals from antibiotics/thyroid/bisphosphonates by at least 2–4 hours.

=== With antibiotics (amoxicillin and class counselling) ===
Probiotics: may help reduce antibiotic-associated diarrhoea; give ≥2 hours away from the antibiotic dose.
Zinc lozenges: separate from some antibiotics; for amoxicillin, 2-hour separation is practical if GI upset occurs.
Multivitamins containing iron/calcium/magnesium: separate by 2 hours from many antibiotics (critical for tetracyclines/quinolones; still sensible with amoxicillin if patient takes high-dose minerals).

=== With warfarin ===
Avoid starting high-dose vitamin E, high-dose fish oil, garlic tablets, ginkgo, or ginseng without INR monitoring — bleeding risk may rise.
Avoid vitamin K supplements and “greens powders” unless diet is consistent and INR is monitored.
Cranberry concentrated products: conflicting data; if used, keep intake steady and monitor INR.
Prefer pharmacist-reviewed products over unknown herbal blends.

=== With metformin ===
Vitamin B12: long-term metformin users may need B12 level checks; supplement if deficient.
Chromium / cinnamon “diabetes supplements”: do not replace metformin; monitor glucose if patient insists on use.
Alcohol-containing tonics: discourage with metformin.

=== With omeprazole / PPIs ===
Calcium: calcium citrate is often better absorbed than calcium carbonate when acid is suppressed.
Magnesium: long-term PPIs can lower magnesium — check levels if cramps/arrhythmia; supplement if low.
Vitamin B12: prolonged acid suppression may reduce B12 absorption — monitor in long-term users.
Iron: separate oral iron from omeprazole timing when possible; consider alternative formulations if absorption is poor.

=== With atorvastatin ===
Red yeast rice: contains statin-like compounds — avoid combining with atorvastatin (duplicate therapy / myopathy risk).
Coenzyme Q10: sometimes used for statin-associated muscle symptoms; evidence mixed — optional adjunct, not a cure for myopathy. Stop statin and seek care if dark urine or severe weakness.
Vitamin D: correct deficiency per labs; does not remove need for statin safety monitoring.
Grapefruit-containing “detox” supplements: avoid with atorvastatin.

=== With NSAIDs (ibuprofen / diclofenac) ===
Fish oil high-dose + NSAID: additive bleed risk — caution.
Licorice root supplements: may worsen blood pressure / fluid retention with NSAIDs — avoid.
Take NSAIDs with food; antacids may be used for dyspepsia but seek review if pain persists.

=== Practical “what can I take?” examples ===
Cold/flu while on warfarin: prefer paracetamol for pain/fever; avoid ibuprofen; check any multi-ingredient cold packs for aspirin/NSAID.
Amoxicillin course and gut comfort: probiotic 2 hours after capsules; yogurt with live cultures is acceptable food support.
Metformin and tiredness: check B12 before blind energising supplements.
PPI long-term: review need for calcium/magnesium/B12 monitoring rather than random megadoses.
