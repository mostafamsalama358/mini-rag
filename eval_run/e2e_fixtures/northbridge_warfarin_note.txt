NORTHBRIDGE CLINICAL NOTE — Anticoagulant Interactions (E2E Test Corpus)
Document ID: NB-WARF-NOTE-E2E
Audience: Pharmacists and clinicians

Warfarin has a narrow therapeutic index. Many medicines potentiate its anticoagulant effect and raise bleeding risk.

NSAIDs including ibuprofen and naproxen can increase the INR effect of warfarin and the risk of gastrointestinal bleeding. Concurrent use should be avoided unless a clinician explicitly authorises it and arranges closer INR monitoring.

If ibuprofen must be used briefly in a patient on warfarin:
- Prefer the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration.
- Advise the patient to report black stools, unusual bruising, or prolonged bleeding immediately.
- Recheck INR within 3 to 7 days of starting or stopping the NSAID.

SSRIs combined with warfarin also increase bleeding risk and warrant caution.
Paracetamol at standard doses is generally preferred over ibuprofen for pain relief in patients taking warfarin.
