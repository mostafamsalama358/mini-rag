5. Warnings and precautions
BLACK BOX / serious warnings: • HEPATOTOXICITY:
- Acetaminophen has been associated with cases of acute liver failure, at times resulting in liver transplant and death. Most of the cases of liver injury are associated with the use of acetaminophen at doses that exceed 4000 milligrams per day, and often involve more than one acetaminophen-containing product.
• The warning also emphasizes the risk of allergic reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN).
Warnings: • If used for Migraine , you can use FEWMIG 
• Avoid:
- Many OTC cold, flu, and pain products contain acetaminophen. Check labels to avoid accidental duplication and overdose.
• Avoid alcohol. Alcohol may increase the risk of hepatotoxicity.
• Take with or without food.
• Avoid foods rich in carbohydrates, and pectin including jellies, cabbage, Brussels sprouts and broccoli as PARACETAMOL may interact with these foods.

Patient education: • CRITICAL:
- Check all your medicines. Many prescription and OTC cold/flu/pain medicines contain acetaminophen. Adding them together can cause a deadly overdose.
• Maximum Dose:
- Never take more than 4000 mg (4 grams) in one day. Taking more can cause severe liver damage, leading to liver failure or death.
• Alcohol:
- Do not take this medicine if you consume 3 or more alcoholic drinks per day. This increases your risk of liver damage.
• Overdose is an emergency. 
• Early symptoms (nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite) may seem like the flu. Seek immediate medical help even if you feel okay.

10. Possible side effects
Major: None
Minor: Hepatotoxicity - dose dependent, can be fatal (rare)*
Hypersensitivity Reactions - rash, urticaria (<0.01%)*
Gastrointestinal discomfort - nausea/vomiting, epigastric pain (1-3%)*
Blood Disorders - e.g., thrombocytopenia, neutropenia; with chronic overdose (rare)*
Renal Toxicity - analgesic nephropathy; with chronic, excessive use (rare)*
