1. What Controloc 20 is used for
Catalog uses: peptic ulcer.proton pump inhibitor
Approved uses (material sheet): Treatment and maintenance of healing of erosive esophagitis (EE) associated with GERD - Adults (Oral): 40 mg once daily for up to 8 weeks. Maintenance: 40 mg once daily , (IV): 40 mg once daily via IV infusion for 7 to 10 days*
Treatment of pathological hypersecretory conditions (e.g., Zollinger Ellison syndrome) - Adults (Oral): Starting dose 40 mg twice daily; adjust based on acid output , (IV): 40 mg once daily via IV infusion for 7 to 10 days*
Off-label mentions (material sheet): Stress ulcer prophylaxis in critically ill patients (IV formulation)*
Treatment of non erosive reflux disease (NERD)*
As part of H. pylori eradication therapy - (not a first line PPI choice)*

5. Warnings and precautions
BLACK BOX / serious warnings (material sheet): • Pantoprazole does not carry an FDA Black Box Warning. However, the prescribing information contains WARNINGS and PRECAUTIONS for:
- Acute Tubulointerstitial Nephritis
- Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea
- Bone Fracture
- Severe Hypomagnesemia
- Vitamin B12 Deficiency
Notes (material sheet): • Avoid:
-Concomitant use with rilpivirine-containing products (due to potential for HIV treatment failure).
• The (PPIs) drugs cause hypomagnesemia, but this does not happen until after (3 months) of using the (PPIs). It is necessary to monitor the magnesium in the blood after 3 months of using the (PPIs).
• Hypomagnesemia may lead to hypocalcemia and/or hypokalemia and may exacerbate underlying hypocalcemia in at-risk patients; in most patients, treatment of hypomagnesemia required magnesium replacement and discontinuation of the PPI

Patient education excerpt: • Timing:
- Take the tablet once daily, before a meal (typically before breakfast). Swallow the delayed-release tablet whole; do not crush, split, or chew.
• What to Report to Your Doctor:
- Diarrhea that is watery or bloody.
- Dizziness, fast or irregular heartbeat, jitteriness, muscle spasms (signs of low magnesium).
- Joint pain, wrist, or hip pain (signs of bone issues).
- New or worsening kidney disease symptoms (e.g., change in urine output).
• Long-Term Use:
- Use the lowest dose and shortest duration of therapy appropriate for your condition. Do not take for longer than prescribed.

6. Interactions (from NEW DRUG INTERACTION.xlsx)
- Pantoprazole + DIPYRIDAMOLE: Risk: Minimal interaction (CYP2C19 sparing). Type: Negligible. Monitoring: Routine follow-up. Dosage Adjustments: None required. Alternative: First-line PPI. Patient Warnings/Management: No special precautions.
- Pantoprazole + SOTALOL: Risk: Bradycardia exacerbation; Type: Pharmacodynamic (additive HR reduction); Monitoring: 24h Holter + exercise tolerance; Dosage: Avoid IV pantoprazole; Alternative: Sucralfate; Patient Warnings: Check AM pulse; withhold if <50 bpm.
- Pantoprazole + METOPROLOL TARTRATE: Risk: Exercise-induced hypotension; Type: Pharmacodynamic (blunted compensatory response); Monitoring: Treadmill BP test; Dosage: Pantoprazole min effective dose; Alternative: Aluminum hydroxide; Patient Warnings: Avoid sudden standing; compression stockings.
- Pantoprazole + METOPROLOL: Risk: Exercise-induced hypotension; Type: Pharmacodynamic (blunted compensatory response); Monitoring: Treadmill BP test + orthostatic checks; Dosage: Pantoprazole min effective dose; Alternative: Aluminum hydroxide antacids; Patient Warnings: Avoid sudden standing; compression stockings.
- Pantoprazole + PROPRANOLOL: Risk: Masked hypoglycemia; Type: Pharmacodynamic (additive β₂-blockade); Monitoring: Fasting glucose + hypoglycemia symptoms; Dosage: Pantoprazole avoid IV route; Alternative: Magaldrate; Patient Warnings: Carry glucose tablets; recognize tremor/sweating.
- Pantoprazole + TIMOLOL: Risk: Masked bradycardia; Type: Pharmacodynamic (blunted compensatory response); Monitoring: 24h Holter if HR<55 bpm; Dosage: Pantoprazole min effective dose; Alternative: Antacids PRN; Patient Warnings: Check AM pulse; withhold if <50 bpm.
- Pantoprazole + ACEBUTOLOL: Risk: Exercise-induced AV block; Type: Pharmacodynamic (additive conduction delay); Monitoring: Stress test ECG; Dosage: Pantoprazole avoid IV; Alternative: Magaldrate; Patient Warnings: Stop activity if dizziness; compression garments.
- Pantoprazole + ATENOLOL: Risk: Cold extremity exacerbation; Type: Pharmacodynamic (peripheral vasoconstriction); Monitoring: Digital thermography + capillary refill; Dosage: Avoid IV pantoprazole; Alternative: Sucralfate; Patient Warnings: Wear thermal gloves; avoid caffeine.
- Pantoprazole + BETAXOLOL: Risk: Masked bronchospasm; Type: Pharmacodynamic (β₂-blockade synergy); Monitoring: Peak flow pre/post dose; Dosage: Pantoprazole min effective dose; Alternative: Magaldrate; Patient Warnings: Keep rescue inhaler; report wheezing.
- Pantoprazole + BISOPROLOL FUMARATE: Risk: Exercise-induced AV block; Type: Pharmacodynamic (additive conduction delay); Monitoring: Stress test ECG + orthostatic BP; Dosage: Avoid IV pantoprazole; Alternative: Calcium-based antacids; Patient Warnings: Stop activity if dizzy; compression stockings.
- Pantoprazole + BISOPROLOL: Risk: Exercise-induced AV block; Type: Pharmacodynamic (additive conduction delay); Monitoring: Stress test ECG; Dosage: Avoid IV pantoprazole; Alternative: Calcium antacids; Patient Warnings: Stop activity if dizzy; compression stockings.
- Pantoprazole + CARVEDILOL: Risk: Heart failure exacerbation; Type: Pharmacodynamic (blunted compensatory response); Monitoring: Daily weights + BNP; Dosage: Pantoprazole min effective dose; Alternative: Magaldrate; Patient Warnings: Weigh daily; report +2kg/24hr edema.

7. Pregnancy
Web-supplemented pregnancy guidance: Generally considered acceptable if acid suppression needed (PPI class; use when benefit outweighs risk).

8. Breastfeeding
Web-supplemented breastfeeding guidance: Small amounts may appear in milk; usually compatible for short courses — confirm with clinician.

9. Supplements / cross-selling
Catalog cross-selling: None listed
Supportive tips: separate minerals from quinolones/tetracyclines by 2–4 hours; probiotics may be timed away from antibiotics; ORS with antidiarrheals when dehydrated.
